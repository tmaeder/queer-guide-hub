# Magnus Hirschfeld

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

German physician and campaigner who connected research on sexuality and gender with organised opposition to the criminalisation of same-sex relationships.


## Research and reform

Magnus Hirschfeld helped found the Scientific-Humanitarian Committee in Berlin in 1897. The organisation campaigned against the criminalisation of sexual activity between men, including through petitions against Paragraph 175. Hirschfeld argued that understanding sexual diversity should change how the law treated people.


## An institution with several roles

In 1919 he established the Institute for Sexual Science in Berlin. Research, consultation, education and support existed together there. The institute matters in queer and trans history because it made an institutional space for subjects and people that established medicine and law often treated with hostility.


## Read the period’s language carefully

Hirschfeld’s concepts emerged from the sexology of his time. Their categories do not map perfectly onto present-day identities, and his authority as a researcher should not be treated as proof that every theory or classification was right. A history can recognise his campaign for reform while examining the limits of its scientific assumptions.


## Persecution and destruction

As a Jewish public figure associated with sexual reform, Hirschfeld became a target of Nazi hostility. In 1933 the institute was attacked and its collections were plundered; books and papers were destroyed in the public book burnings. Hirschfeld was abroad and did not return to Germany. He died in Nice in 1935.


## What the archive makes visible

The history is not only about the destruction of a building. It concerns the loss of records, networks and possibilities for care, as well as the lives of people connected to the institute. Surviving sources allow readers to ask how knowledge was produced, who sought help, and how research became part of a struggle over legal and social recognition.
