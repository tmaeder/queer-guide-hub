# Medical Transition

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Medical care that a person may choose to help align aspects of their body with their gender, including hormones or surgery.


## One part of a person’s life

Medical transition can be discussed alongside social changes, such as a name or presentation, and legal changes, such as documents. These do not form a compulsory sequence. People may pursue some changes, postpone others or choose not to use medical care at all.


## There is no single finish line

Different people have different goals. One person may want hormone treatment without surgery; another may seek a particular procedure. A person’s gender does not depend on completing a list of interventions, and there is no universal point at which every trans person is finished.


## An informed conversation

A useful clinical discussion considers goals, potential benefits, risks, alternatives and uncertainties. It should also make space for questions about fertility, recovery, ongoing care and the practicalities of treatment. These are topics for discussion, not a substitute for an individual assessment.


## Access and context

Services, eligibility rules, costs and waiting times differ between healthcare systems and change over time. A general definition should not promise access or describe one country’s process as universal. Where a guide gives practical details, those details need a named jurisdiction, a checked source and a review date.


## Source candidates
