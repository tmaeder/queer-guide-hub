# Camp

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

A sensibility that finds pleasure and meaning in artifice, theatricality and exaggeration, often with a knowing relationship to taste and convention.


## More than “over the top”

Camp can turn a dramatic entrance, an extravagant costume or an emphatic performance into something both funny and affecting. The point is not simply that something is large or colourful. A camp reading notices style, excess and the tension between taking something seriously and enjoying its performance.


## A contested idea

Susan Sontag’s 1964 essay Notes on “Camp” helped bring the term into wider critical discussion. It is an influential account rather than the final definition. The Metropolitan Museum of Art’s 2019 exhibition explored camp through fashion, including irony, parody, artifice and theatrical exaggeration.


## Queer associations

Camp has important associations with queer culture, including ways of playing with conventional masculinity, femininity and respectability. It can create recognition between people who share a reference or a sense of humour. That does not mean every queer person enjoys camp, or that every camp performance tells us its performer’s identity.


## How to describe it

Instead of calling an artwork camp and stopping there, identify what creates the effect: an intentionally extravagant silhouette, a solemn delivery of an absurd line, or a familiar convention pushed beyond its usual limits. Different audiences may read the same work differently. That disagreement is part of the term’s history and usefulness.
