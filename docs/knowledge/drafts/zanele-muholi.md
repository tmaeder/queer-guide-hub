# Zanele Muholi

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

South African artist and visual activist whose portraits and self-portraits build a sustained record of Black queer lives and challenge how those lives are represented.


## An archive made with people

Zanele Muholi’s photography is a continuing project of visibility and historical record. Faces and Phases brings named participants into a photographic archive of Black queer life in South Africa. The encounter is more specific than an anonymous image standing for a demographic group: the person pictured has a name, a context and a relationship to the work.


## Portraits and self-portraits

In Faces and Phases, recurring portraits make time and change part of the archive. In Somnyama Ngonyama, Muholi turns to self-portraiture, using everyday objects and deliberate visual arrangements to confront questions of race, labour, representation and power. The two projects offer different ways of asking who controls the image and how a viewer learns to look.


## Why “visual activist” matters

Muholi describes their practice through visual activism. The work addresses exclusion and violence, but it also insists on dignity, beauty, intimacy and ordinary presence. Reducing it to pictures of suffering would miss what is being built: a record in which the people represented can appear as more than a social problem.


## Looking with context

A caption, a participant’s account or the sequence of a series can change how an image is understood. Museum presentation should not erase those relationships. Likewise, an image of one participant should not be used as a stock representation of all queer people in South Africa.


## An entry point

Begin with a named series and read the accompanying accounts of its making. Notice the different roles of photographer, participant and viewer, and the ways the series changes over time. This gives a clearer introduction to Muholi’s contribution than a list of exhibitions or a general statement that photography increases visibility.
