# Judith Butler

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Philosopher whose work asks how gender becomes socially recognisable and how norms shape whose lives receive protection and recognition.


## What gender performativity asks

Judith Butler’s Gender Trouble, published in 1990, questions the idea that gender is a fixed inner truth merely expressed through outward behaviour. The idea of performativity concerns the repeated acts, conventions and social expectations through which gender comes to seem natural. It is not simply a claim that people put on a costume or can change their circumstances by an act of will.


## Why repetition matters

A familiar example can help open the question: everyday expectations about dress, voice or conduct may make some people’s genders immediately legible while placing others under scrutiny. That example illustrates a line of inquiry, not a complete account of Butler’s argument. Bodies, institutions and unequal power remain part of the discussion.


## A body of work beyond one phrase

Bodies That Matter develops questions about bodies and the norms through which they are understood. Later work considers vulnerability, violence, interdependence and the public recognition of loss. These concerns connect questions about gender to wider debates about which people are treated as fully human and which lives are regarded as worth protecting.


## Influence and disagreement

Butler’s work has influenced queer theory, feminist thought, literary studies and political philosophy. It has also generated sustained criticism. A useful introduction should give readers enough of the argument to understand a disagreement, rather than presenting “gender is a performance” as a slogan that settles it.


## A route into the writing

Begin with a specific question: what makes an identity recognisable, and what happens to people who do not fit that recognition? Read definitions in their surrounding argument and distinguish Butler’s own words from a later writer’s interpretation. Dense language becomes easier to assess when it is connected to the concrete problem the text is trying to examine.
