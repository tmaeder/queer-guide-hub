# Bisexual

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Attraction to more than one gender, which need not happen in the same way, at the same time or to the same degree.


## What the word describes

Bisexual describes a capacity for attraction, rather than a count of someone’s past or current partners. A person can be bisexual while single, in a long-term relationship, or without having had a sexual relationship. Their partner’s gender does not turn that identity into a different orientation.


## Making room for variation

Some people experience romantic and sexual attraction differently. Others notice that the intensity or pattern of attraction changes over time. Neither experience creates an obligation to demonstrate equal attraction to different genders. The word can include attraction to nonbinary people; it does not require a belief that only two genders exist.


## Related labels

Bi, bi+ and bisexual are used in overlapping ways. Pansexual, omnisexual and queer may also describe attraction to more than one gender, but people choose these words for different reasons. A useful glossary explains those possible meanings without deciding that one label cancels out another. Ask which word someone uses rather than assigning one from their relationship history.


## An everyday example

A bisexual person attending an event with a partner of a different gender is still part of the bisexual community. Describing the couple as straight can erase that identity. If orientation matters to the conversation, use the person’s own description; if it does not, there is no need to turn an introduction into a discussion of sexuality.
