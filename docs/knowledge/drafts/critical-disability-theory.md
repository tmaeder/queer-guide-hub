# Critical Disability Theory

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

A range of approaches that examines disability through power, social organisation, lived experience and the norms used to define acceptable bodies and minds.


## Changing the question

Instead of asking only how an individual body differs from a medical norm, critical disability theory also asks how environments and institutions create exclusion. A staircase, a hiring rule or an assumption about communication can make participation harder for some people. These are illustrative questions, not a claim that all disability can be explained by barriers alone.


## More than one model

The field includes disagreements about bodies, impairment, dependence, care and political strategy. It should not be reduced to a simple contest in which medicine is always wrong and society is the only problem. People can need healthcare and want structural change at the same time.


## Connections with queer thought

Disability and queer scholarship can both question how a preferred kind of body or life becomes an unquestioned standard. Their overlap includes attention to gender, sexuality, race and class. Sharing a question does not mean disabled and queer people have identical experiences, or that one identity automatically includes the other.


## A useful reading habit

Ask whose experience a theory centres and whose it leaves out. An account based only on visible physical disability may miss chronic illness, cognitive disability or fluctuating access needs. Theory becomes more useful when it helps describe a concrete exclusion and makes space for people to explain what participation would require.
