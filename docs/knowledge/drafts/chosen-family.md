# Chosen Family

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

People who build an ongoing relationship of family through care, commitment and belonging, whether or not they are related by birth or marriage.


## Family made through practice

Chosen family can include friends, partners, former partners, mentors and other important people. What makes the relationship familial is the meaning and commitment of those involved. Everyday acts such as sharing meals, showing up during illness or celebrating an achievement can be part of that care.


## More than a response to rejection

Chosen family has particular importance in queer lives shaped by exclusion from relatives or institutions. But rejection is not a requirement. A person can be close to their family of origin and have a chosen family too. Joy, shared history and the wish to build a life together matter alongside survival and support.


## Ask who belongs

A support network should be described by the person receiving support. The friend who brings meals may be more relevant than a distant relative; someone labelled a family member may not be someone they want involved. Ask whom they consider family and what information they want shared. Do not assume that affection gives permission to disclose private details.


## Belonging and boundaries

Calling a relationship family does not remove the need for boundaries or make care limitless. People can discuss what they can offer, how to ask for help and how responsibilities are shared. Chosen family is a way of making meaningful connections, not a promise that one group of people can meet every need.
