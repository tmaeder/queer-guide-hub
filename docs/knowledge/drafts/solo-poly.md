# Solo Poly

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

An approach to polyamory that centres personal autonomy rather than organising life around a primary or nesting couple.


## Independent does not mean unconnected

Someone practising solo polyamory may have loving, committed relationships while choosing not to share a home, finances or a primary partnership. The word solo describes how they organise their life, not the depth of their feelings or the number of people they happen to be dating.


## A relationship structure, not an orientation

Polyamory involves the possibility of more than one intimate relationship with the knowledge and agreement of the people involved. Solo poly describes one way of arranging those relationships. It does not identify the genders someone is attracted to, and it is not a synonym for being single or dating without agreements.


## Talk about the actual arrangements

A label cannot answer whether partners expect regular dates, emergency support, exclusivity in a particular activity, or notice when circumstances change. Those expectations need discussion. Independence does not remove responsibilities created by agreements, and an agreement can be revisited when someone’s needs change.


## Avoid the shortcut

Calling a solo-poly person afraid of commitment mistakes one model of commitment for the only possible one. Equally, independence should not be presented as a more advanced way to relate. The useful question is whether the people involved understand and freely choose the relationship they are making.
