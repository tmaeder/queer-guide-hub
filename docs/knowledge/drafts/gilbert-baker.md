# Gilbert Baker

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Artist, activist and flag-maker who created the rainbow flag in 1978 as a public symbol of queer pride and collective belonging.


## Making a symbol together

Gilbert Baker conceived the rainbow flag in San Francisco, where volunteers helped dye and sew the first flags for the 1978 Gay Freedom Day celebration. The familiar symbol began as physical work with fabric, colour and a community of people. Remembering that work keeps its history from becoming a story of an image appearing fully formed.


## From eight colours to six

The earliest design used eight colours. The widely recognised six-stripe version developed later. When discussing a particular flag, distinguish Baker’s original design, subsequent versions and later flags created by other designers. Their histories can connect without being identical.


## A flag that travels

Baker continued making flags on a large scale. The rainbow became something people could wear, raise over a building or carry in a march. Its usefulness was partly in that portability: it could turn a personal expression of pride into a shared public sign.


## Art and organising

Flag-making linked Baker’s artistic practice to activism. The work invites attention to the making and circulation of symbols, not only to their appearance. A flag can gather people around a shared claim while leaving room for differences between them. It can also prompt debate about who a symbol includes and what it asks institutions to do.


## Reading the legacy

Baker died in 2017. The flag’s continuing presence does not mean its meaning is fixed or that every place displaying it is welcoming in practice. His biography is an opportunity to connect a familiar object with the people, labour and organising that produced it. When reproducing an image, identify which version is shown and credit its documented maker.
