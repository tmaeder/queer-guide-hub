# Single

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Not currently in a partnership as a person defines it; being single can be a circumstance, a preference or a valued way of life.


## A status with different meanings

Single is often used to mean not married or not in a romantic relationship. Those definitions do not always line up. An unmarried person may have a long-term partner; a person who lives alone may have several close relationships. Context matters, especially on forms, dating profiles and invitations.


## A full life, not a waiting room

Friendship, family, work, pleasure and community do not become less meaningful because someone is single. Some people want a partnership, some do not, and some are unsure. A person should not have to frame their life as preparation for a future couple in order to be understood.


## Do not infer availability

Being single does not mean wanting a date, a sexual encounter or matchmaking advice. It also does not tell you someone’s orientation, sexual experience or interest in romance. An invitation can be made respectfully, but the answer has to come from the person, not from their relationship status.


## Different from solo polyamory

Some solo-poly people describe themselves as single, while others have established relationships and do not. Solo polyamory concerns autonomy and relationship organisation; single concerns a status or self-description. Use the person’s own language rather than treating these as interchangeable categories.
