# Non-Binary Terms of Endearment

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Affectionate names for nonbinary partners and loved ones, chosen according to the person’s preferences rather than a fixed gendered vocabulary.


## Start with the person

There is no official set of nonbinary pet names. Some people like familiar words such as love, darling or sweetheart. Others enjoy a gendered word, a private nickname or no pet name at all. Nonbinary describes gender; it does not predict how someone wants to be addressed.


## Affection and relationship titles

A term of endearment is not quite the same as a relationship title. “My love” expresses affection; “my partner” describes a relationship to someone else. Some couples choose different words for private conversation and public introductions. Ask what works in each setting instead of assuming one answer applies everywhere.


## Pronouns are another question

Knowing someone’s pronouns does not tell you their preferred pet names. Similarly, a nickname does not supply their pronouns. Names, pronouns, titles and endearments can each have their own meaning. It is more useful to ask a small, relevant question than to request a complete explanation of someone’s gender.


## A simple conversation

“Do you like being called sweetheart?” leaves room for a yes, a no or a different suggestion. If someone changes their preference, use the new one without making them defend it. The point of affectionate language is to make the other person feel recognised, not to prove that a word is theoretically gender-neutral.
