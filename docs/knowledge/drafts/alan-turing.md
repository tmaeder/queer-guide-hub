# Alan Turing

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Mathematician and computing pioneer whose work on computation and wartime codebreaking sits alongside a history of state persecution for a relationship with another man.


## A question about what machines can do

Alan Turing’s 1936 paper On Computable Numbers examined what it means to carry out a calculation by following definite rules. The abstract machine associated with his name helped make the limits and possibilities of computation precise. It was a mathematical account, not a description of a laptop built decades before its time.


## Wartime work in a wider effort

During the Second World War, Turing worked at Bletchley Park, including on German naval Enigma. His contributions belonged to a large and collaborative codebreaking effort. Keeping that setting visible avoids replacing a complex history with a story in which one isolated genius solved everything alone.


## After the war

Turing continued work connected to electronic computing and artificial intelligence, and also investigated mathematical patterns in biological development. His interests were broader than the wartime episode for which he is often remembered. Reading across those areas gives a more useful sense of his contribution than treating the Turing test as his only lasting idea.


## Prosecution and its consequences

In 1952, Turing was prosecuted under laws criminalising sexual activity between men. He received a sentence involving hormonal treatment rather than imprisonment. The prosecution damaged his life and work. His scientific achievement and the injustice of that treatment belong in the same biography; neither needs to be reduced to an illustration of the other.


## Remembering a person, not only a symbol

Turing died in 1954. Later public recognition included a royal pardon, but recognition after death did not undo the harm inflicted by the law. His history can prompt questions about the institutions that exclude people even while benefiting from their work. It also invites readers to encounter his actual mathematical and scientific ideas, rather than knowing him only through a film or a tragic ending.
