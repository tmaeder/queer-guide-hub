# Testosterone Therapy

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

Treatment using testosterone, including its use in masculinising gender-affirming care; the aims and monitoring depend on the person and clinical context.


## What the term covers

Testosterone can be used in gender-affirming care to support physical changes associated with masculinisation. It also has uses outside trans healthcare, including treatment for some people with testosterone deficiency. A shared medication does not mean every patient has the same goals or treatment plan.


## Individual goals matter

Trans men and some nonbinary people may seek testosterone, while others do not. The choice does not establish how valid someone’s gender is. A consultation can address the changes a person wants, changes they do not want, and the limits of what treatment can predict.


## Treatment requires clinical context

Preparation, formulation, monitoring and follow-up are medical matters. A glossary should help readers understand the conversation rather than prescribe a dose or suggest that a regimen found online is appropriate for them. Relevant questions include expected changes, fertility, other medicines and how follow-up will be arranged.


## Different ways of taking it

Injectable and transdermal preparations are among the options described in clinical guidance. Availability and product instructions vary. The form of testosterone is one part of a treatment decision; it is not a measure of how committed someone is to transition.
