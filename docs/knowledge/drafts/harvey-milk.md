# Harvey Milk

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

San Francisco organiser and elected official who used local politics to press for gay rights and broader participation in public life.


## From neighbourhood to public office

Harvey Milk’s political work grew from San Francisco’s neighbourhood life and organising. His election to the Board of Supervisors in 1977 made him one of the first openly gay elected officials in the United States. It should not be described as the first such election without qualification: other openly gay officials had already held office.


## Representation as practical politics

Milk connected the visibility of a gay candidate to questions about who could be heard in local government. Campaigns, neighbourhood businesses and coalitions mattered alongside speeches. A public office offered a way to turn organising into decisions about the city rather than leaving queer people outside its political institutions.


## Opposing the Briggs Initiative

In 1978, Milk was among the campaigners opposing California’s Proposition 6, commonly called the Briggs Initiative. The measure threatened the employment of gay and lesbian teachers and others associated with supporting them. Its defeat belonged to a broad campaign; no single person can account for the whole result.


## Assassination and memory

Milk and Mayor George Moscone were assassinated at City Hall in November 1978. Their deaths became part of the city’s political history and queer public memory. The violence should not obscure the work that preceded it or turn Milk’s life into a single ending.


## Reading beyond the icon

A useful introduction brings together his organising, public service and the particular conflicts of 1970s San Francisco. It also distinguishes contemporary records from later films, commemorations and simplified slogans. Milk’s place in queer history is more informative when readers can see what he tried to change, whom he worked with and how local politics made those efforts possible.
