# Pride Flag

Candidate English draft · not approved or published.

A flag used to express queer identity, solidarity or belonging; the rainbow flag is one widely recognised example among many community flags.


## A symbol people can carry

Gilbert Baker created the rainbow flag in San Francisco in 1978, working with others to make the first flags. Its colours offered a shared public symbol for a movement. The familiar six-stripe version developed from an earlier design with eight colours.


## One flag, several histories

The rainbow flag and later identity-specific flags have distinct makers and contexts. A new design may recognise a particular community or address who has been left out of a broader symbol. They should be described with their own provenance rather than treated as anonymous variations of a single original.


## A symbol is not a guarantee

A flag in a window may communicate welcome or solidarity, but it does not establish how a place handles accessibility, discrimination, privacy or safety. Those practices need their own evidence. A person carrying a flag may be identifying themself, supporting someone else or taking part in a shared event.


## When reproducing a design

Name the design you are showing and check its creator and documented meaning. Avoid inventing colour symbolism from appearance alone. For a historical article, distinguish an original flag from a later reconstruction or adaptation. That small amount of context makes the image more useful than a decorative rainbow beside an unrelated paragraph.
