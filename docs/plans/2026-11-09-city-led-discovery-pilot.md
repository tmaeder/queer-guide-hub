# City-led discovery pilot

Travel and community have equal weight. A visitor can begin with a city, find something to do there, and discover a public way to connect. Search and directory access stay in the opening view for visitors arriving with a specific task.

## First slice

Feature Zürich in the homepage opening, with its existing city photograph and transit network as a fallback. Link to the city guide, a dated public event from the existing city-scoped feed, and Regenbogenhaus. Link to the Zürich event directory when there is no eligible event. The city guide and venue detail already provide paths into trip planning. Use the existing product design tokens, localized navigation, and content visibility policies.

## Follow-through

City guides bring the photo and up to two local listings into the opening overview, with the safety verdict first and the map and practical details below. Events leads with a near-term listing from the current feed during default browsing. Shared filters, search, timeline, and map views go straight to their results and controls.

Meet people opens with local gatherings and community spaces when available, plus a public group. The connection map has a shortcut in the opening and loads when approached. Travel opens with the published cover or a seasonal destination and its reason to go. An active trip gets a direct continuation link, and booking links open the booking section.

The Venues directory pairs its title with a real featured place during default browsing and puts results before guides and recommendations. Venue pages open with the description and available photo, with shortcuts to location and hours and a dated event when one is available.

Test whether visitors can find one thing they would do and one way to connect. Compare that experience with time to reach search, event and venue detail opens, and actions that carry an idea into a trip. Review at mobile and desktop widths, with missing imagery, sparse listings, and signed-in travel context.
