# Verified trip invitations

Trip invitation email is backed by an authenticated invitation and review flow. It does not infer an inviter from a membership row or send mail for arbitrary membership imports. Marketing and newsletter delivery remain blocked by the Cloudflare provider capability.

## Member experience

Trip sharing now includes an existing-member username invitation form, with viewer/editor access and revocation controls for pending invitations. Plans links to `/trips/invitations`. That page requires sign-in before loading private invitation details and requires an explicit accept or decline. Opening an email or the page never accepts an invitation. Acceptance opens the trip and invalidates canonical trip queries; declined invitations grant no access. Member query caches include the signed-in account.

Trip invitation email is a separate opt-in in account settings and defaults off. Inviting a member records their current email choice; enabling it later does not backfill invitation mail. Opt-out permanently cancels older pending email sources and queued snapshots without overwriting the original opt-in proof; turning the switch back on cannot revive them. Live leases retain receipt persistence while a cancellation marker vetoes further submissions. The email contains only a generic invitation notice and a fixed review link. Trip names, locations, dates, participant names, invitation IDs and acceptance credentials stay out of its subject/body. The signed-in review page may show the invited trip's title and inviter name after verifying ownership and current source eligibility.

## Backend and source proof

`trip-invitations` owns its authorization (`verify_jwt=false`). Member operations always verify a real Auth user and derive the actor from that user; internal/admin queue credentials cannot select a member actor. Service-only RPCs are not directly callable by members or anonymous clients. Request validation rejects arbitrary actor IDs, addresses, content and URLs.

- GET returns the member's current pending invitations. GET with a valid `trip_id` returns management data only for a current accepted editor or actual trip owner; other members receive no management data. Lists are bounded to 100 sources.
- POST `create` accepts `trip_id`, exact existing-member `username`, and `role` (`viewer` or `editor`). It checks current inviter authority and blocks, creates/adopts a pending membership, and stores the invitation proof atomically. An actual owner/accepted editor may invite; pending members and viewers may not. A member cannot assign the owner role through an invitation.
- POST `respond` accepts `invitation_id` and `decision` (`accept` or `decline`). Only that invitation's recipient may respond. Acceptance retains the originally offered role and sets membership acceptance; decline removes the pending membership. Repeated identical decisions are idempotent. An unavailable, expired, blocked or revoked source cannot grant access.
- POST `revoke` permits the original inviter while still authorized, or the actual trip owner. It removes pending access only. It cannot remove an already accepted member; losing a simultaneous race to acceptance returns a conflict.
- POST `tick`, optionally with `source_id`, requires the existing internal/admin gate and queues at most five eligible emails through the shared outbox. The response is 202 queued/skipped, never a claim of delivery. Cloudflare must be configured before queueing.

Invitations retain original inviter/recipient membership IDs, offered role and source identity. Both memberships and current inviter authority must still match. Leaving/rejoining, changing the recipient/trip/role, acceptance, decline, revocation, either party being blocked, the recipient blocking the trip owner, account erasure, completed/archived trips, or trip deletion invalidate delivery. Pending invitation review expires after seven days. The optional email snapshot expires after 24 hours. A deliberate fresh invitation after expiry/revocation creates a new membership/source; a retry of a current invitation returns its existing identity.

A membership trigger closes an existing pending-member privilege gap: a pending recipient may change acceptance only, cannot change their role or membership identity, and cannot accept a modern invitation whose source is no longer actionable. Other members cannot set that recipient's acceptance. Authenticated writes cannot assign the owner role to someone other than the actual trip owner. Trusted service/bootstrap writes retain their existing boundary, while public invitation RPCs independently enforce the same permission requirements. Existing raw/imported membership records continue to emit operational lifecycle events; they do not automatically acquire email invitation proof.

Creation uses ordered account erasure barriers for both parties before trip/member row locks. These also serialize an inviter's admission budget across different trips. A service-only counter permits at most 20 new invitations per inviter per UTC clock hour; duplicates of a live invitation do not spend the budget, and deleting a trip/source does not reset it. Response/revocation use the account barriers before row locks, matching erasure and lifecycle capture. Source membership updates record terminal accepted/revoked state; resetting a field cannot reactivate a terminal source.

## Delivery, operations and privacy

`trip_invitation` joins DM digests and group mentions in the single possible activity submission allowance per account per UTC day, with the same time-zone-aware quiet hours. Claim and immediate dispatch authorization recheck current source, preferences, primary mailbox and suppression. Enqueue derives the recipient from current Auth. The immutable key is SHA-256 of `trip-invitation:<source UUID>` and does not change with branding or mailbox changes. A changed address cannot resend the same invitation. Retries use the same Worker Durable Object identity; uncertain outcomes keep their daily reservation. A live processing lease stays available to persist a provider receipt already in flight. The database check cannot recall mail already submitted across the external network boundary.

The minute source-queue scheduler uses the existing vault secret and an `email_trip_invitations` admin automation switch. Disable it to stop new queue creation; existing rows still obey current eligibility. Deploy the eighth migration, `trip-invitations`, the changed settings/shared functions, and the UI together. Local verification does not execute the scheduler's enabled network path, deploy infrastructure, or send real email.

Invitation records, admission counters and existing preference/delivery records have RLS and service-only grants. Exports include the account's sent/received invitation metadata and its admission counters, without private trip content or the inviter's membership proof. Recipient/inviter erasure deletes owned sources; recipient outbox erasure also clears source links through the existing shared barrier. Auth/trip deletion cascades source removal. Retention periods for terminal invitations and counters remain a production policy requirement.

This continuation also serializes group mention source capture with both sender and recipient erasure barriers. A real overlapping-transaction contract verifies that a concurrent group post cannot recreate sender-linked metadata after the sender's scrub commits.

## Verification scope

The SQL suite exercises permission and role boundaries, default-off/no-backfill preferences, older settings clients, idempotent creation/decisions/revocation, invitation admission limits, membership privilege escalation, block directions and owner blocks, source expiry/renewal, archive/current mailbox/current inviter checks, quiet hours, opt-out, exports, erasure and RPC privileges. Real overlapping connections test shared DM/group/trip allowances, group source creation versus sender erasure, acceptance versus revocation, and acceptance versus inviter erasure, in addition to the existing journey cases. Backend request contracts reject impersonation/content fields. UI tests cover explicit acceptance, decline, sign-in, failed-load recovery, stale actions, member invite/revoke and independent email preferences.

These fixtures provide the required source columns and authorization boundary stubs, not the application's full migration history. Full deployed trip/profile/Auth compatibility, controlled delivery, inbox placement and measured throughput remain rollout gates. There is no non-member invitation by email address, automatic acceptance, reminder sequence, itinerary-change campaign or arbitrary trip email action in this release.

Local verification on 2026-10-20: all eight email migrations applied to a fresh isolated PostgreSQL database, all eight SQL contract suites passed, and six real overlapping-transaction cases passed. Eighteen shared backend contracts and thirty targeted UI/query-key tests passed. Changed endpoints passed Deno checking, changed UI passed ESLint, the typecheck ratchet reported no new errors (698 existing versus baseline 782), and the production frontend build passed. Security advisor found no issues on the isolated fixture database. Invitation review and sharing fixtures were inspected at desktop/mobile widths (1280/390 pixels), with no horizontal overflow or browser errors. Fixture tabs/files and the local server were removed after verification. No production migration, deployment, enabled scheduler network execution or real mail was performed.
